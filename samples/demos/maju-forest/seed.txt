For a five-person rehearsal, request one fictional participant from each of these five groups:
- Prospective home buyers: consider affordability, access to housing, delivery dates, amenities, and long-term environmental quality.
- Nearby residents: consider access, construction disruption, local services, green space, and opportunities to participate in decisions.
- Conservation researchers: consider baseline evidence, habitat connectivity, species-specific impacts, monitoring, uncertainty, and mitigation effectiveness.
- Infrastructure planners: consider housing need, transport and utility capacity, land allocation, sequencing, environmental commitments, and public accountability.
- Construction implementers: consider safe execution, practical sequencing, workforce and resource constraints, monitoring responsibilities, and changes triggered by new evidence.

These are perspectives to explore, not predetermined positions. Every group may support, question, or conditionally accept parts of the framework. Give participants different priorities within their groups. Do not treat any group as speaking for an actual community, agency, or profession.

Invite participants to ask what the text specifies, what remains undefined, and what evidence would change their view. Allow disagreement and uncertainty. Do not require consensus, a particular environmental conclusion, or a predetermined recommendation.

Candidate questions:
1. Must the corridor be established and its connectivity assessed before each clearing phase, or can construction occur later?
2. How is "moderate displacement" defined, measured, and reviewed for different species?
3. Who publishes baseline and ongoing monitoring, on what schedule, and who is accountable for follow-up?
4. What evidence triggers a pause, redesign, or additional mitigation, and who makes that decision?
5. How would housing timing, infrastructure delivery, and mitigation commitments be considered together if assumptions change?
